Vigan Longganisa Recipe


Description

Vigan Longganisa is a distinct, small, plump pork sausage from the historic heritage city of Vigan in Ilocos Sur. Unlike the sweet hamonado style common elsewhere in the Philippines, Vigan longganisa is famous for being aggressively garlicky, savory, and pleasantly sour thanks to local Ilocos sugarcane vinegar (sukang Iloko).

It is traditionally pan-steamed in a little water, pricked so its savory pork fat renders out, and then fried in its own oil until crisp. A prime component of the classic "Longsilog" breakfast!

Ingredients

  • 1 kg ground pork (70% lean, 30% fat)
  • 2 heads native garlic, finely crushed and minced
  • 1/3 cup sukang Iloko (Ilocos sugarcane vinegar or apple cider vinegar)
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon coarse sea salt
  • 1 tablespoon freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • Sausage hog casings, soaked and rinsed
  • Kitchen twine for tying
  • 1/2 cup water (for cooking)

Steps

  1. In a large mixing bowl, combine the ground pork, crushed garlic, sukang Iloko, soy sauce, sea salt, black pepper, and brown sugar. Mix thoroughly until sticky.
  2. Cover the bowl and let the mixture cure in the refrigerator overnight.
  3. Fit the sausage casing onto a funnel or sausage stuffer nozzle. Gently stuff the meat into the casing, being careful not to overpack.
  4. Tie off with kitchen twine every 2 to 2.5 inches to form plump, bite-sized links. Prick each link lightly with a toothpick to prevent bursting.
  5. To cook: place links in a skillet with 1/2 cup of water over medium heat.
  6. Simmer until the water evaporates completely, allowing the natural pork fat to render in the pan.
  7. Fry the sausages in their own rendered fat for 5 to 8 minutes, rolling frequently until dark golden brown and crispy.
  8. Serve hot with sinangag (garlic fried rice), sunny-side up eggs, and sliced tomatoes.
